Chunking Strategies for RAG Systems

Chunking is the process of splitting long documents into smaller pieces
before embedding them. The chunking strategy has a large effect on
retrieval quality.

Fixed-size chunking splits text every N characters or tokens, optionally
with overlap between consecutive chunks. It is simple and fast but can cut
sentences or ideas in half.

Recursive character splitting tries a sequence of separators (paragraph
breaks, then line breaks, then sentence breaks, then spaces) and only falls
back to a harder split when a piece is still too large. This tends to keep
semantically related text together more often than naive fixed-size
splitting.

Semantic chunking groups sentences or paragraphs based on topical
similarity rather than a fixed character count, producing chunks that are
more internally coherent, which tends to improve retrieval precision at
the cost of extra preprocessing time.

Overlap between chunks (for example, 100-200 characters) helps preserve
context that spans a chunk boundary, which reduces cases where an answer is
split across two poorly-connected chunks.

Choosing chunk size involves a tradeoff: smaller chunks give more precise
retrieval (less irrelevant text per chunk) but may lose surrounding
context; larger chunks preserve context but dilute the embedding with
unrelated content, which can lower retrieval precision.
