Chunking Strategies for Retrieval Systems

The way source documents are split into chunks has a large effect on
retrieval quality. Three common strategies are fixed-size recursive
splitting, token-based splitting, and semantic splitting.

Recursive character splitting divides text using a prioritized list of
separators, such as paragraph breaks, then sentence breaks, then spaces,
until each chunk fits under a target character count. This tends to
preserve natural document structure like paragraphs, and is a reasonable
default for most text.

Token-based splitting divides text based on a fixed number of tokens as
counted by the model's tokenizer, rather than characters. This is useful
when the downstream language model has a strict context window and the
chunk size needs to be predictable in token terms rather than approximate.

Semantic splitting groups sentences together based on embedding similarity,
starting a new chunk when the topic appears to shift. This can produce
chunks that are more topically coherent than fixed-size splitting, at the
cost of extra embedding calls during ingestion and more variable chunk
sizes, which can complicate downstream context-window budgeting.

Overlap between chunks, typically 10-20% of the chunk size, is commonly
used to reduce the chance that a fact gets split across a chunk boundary
and becomes unretrievable in either chunk alone.
